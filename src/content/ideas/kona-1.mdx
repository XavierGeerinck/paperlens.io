# The End of "Probable"

The era of "guessing" is over. Yesterday, **Logical Intelligence** released **Kona 1.0**, the first commercial Energy-Based Model (EBM) for reasoning, alongside a stunning leadership shakeup: Turing Award winner **Yann LeCun** has joined as Founding Chair of the Technical Research Board, and former Binance executive **Patrick Hillmann** has been appointed Chief Strategy Officer.

While GPT-5 and DeepSeek V3 continue to push the boundaries of *explicit* text generation, Kona aims for something different: **implicit, provable correctness**.

## The Core Thesis: Implicit Chain-of-Thought

Kona 1 is the first commercial implementation of the framework proposed in *"Think Consistently, Reason Efficiently: Energy-Based Calibration for Implicit Chain-of-Thought"* (Chen et al., Nov 2025).

Traditional LLMs "think out loud"—generating tokens step-by-step ($x_1, x_2, ...$). If they make a mistake at step 3, the error propagates. Kona, by contrast, thinks in the **continuous latent space** before generating a single word.

- **Explicit CoT**: Discrete, brittle, prone to error cascading.
- **Implicit EBM-CoT**: Continuous, fluid, self-correcting via energy minimization.

## Architecture: The Latent Manifold

Kona separates **Intelligence** (Optimization) from **Language** (Generation).

1.  **Thinking Stage**: The problem is encoded into a continuous "Soft Thought" tensor.
2.  **Reasoning Stage (Langevin Dynamics)**: The system applies an **Energy Function** $E(z)$ that measures logical consistency. It uses gradient descent to "slide" the thought tensor toward a low-energy state (valid solution).
3.  **Generation Stage**: Only when the energy is minimized is the thought "collapsed" (decoded) into text or actions.

## Algorithm: Langevin Calibration

The breakthrough in Kona 1 is **Langevin Calibration**. Instead of sampling the next token, we sample the *meaning* of the solution and refine it against hard logical constraints.

### Why This Solves the "Sudoku Problem"
In discrete solvers (like standard code), you are either "Right" or "Wrong". In Kona's continuous space, a cell can be "80% a 3, 20% a 4". This fluidity allows the gradients to push the system out of local minima that trap discrete solvers.

## Performance

In the demos released on `logicalintelligence.com`, Kona solves "Evil" grade Sudoku puzzles in **14ms** on a single H100 GPU. This is achieved not by being smarter, but by being **differentiable**. It doesn't "search" the tree of possibilities; it "flows" to the answer.
